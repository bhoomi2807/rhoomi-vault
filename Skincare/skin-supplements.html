Five Supplements

A practical overview of what each supplement may support, how it is commonly used, and where expectations should stay realistic.

Astaxanthin

Antioxidant

Early studies suggest possible modest benefits for skin hydration and elasticity, but the evidence is still limited.

Common amount: 4-12 mg daily with a fat-containing meal.

Creatine Monohydrate

Strength + recovery

Well studied for strength, exercise performance, and muscle support. It is not primarily a skincare supplement.

Common amount: 3-5 g daily; timing is not important.

Magnesium

Dietary mineral

Supports normal muscle, nerve, and energy function. Supplementing is most useful when dietary intake is low or deficiency is identified.

Common amount: 100-350 mg of elemental magnesium daily.

Omega-3

EPA + DHA

Supports cardiovascular health and may help regulate inflammation. Choose a product that clearly lists its EPA and DHA amounts.

Common amount: about 1 g combined EPA and DHA daily with food.

Marine Collagen

Skin + connective tissue

Hydrolyzed collagen peptides may modestly improve skin hydration and elasticity after consistent use for several weeks.

Common amount: 2.5-10 g daily.

Before starting

Check with a clinician or pharmacist if you are pregnant, have kidney disease, take prescription medicines, or use blood thinners. Magnesium can interfere with some antibiotics and thyroid medicine; marine collagen is unsuitable for anyone with a fish allergy. Supplements do not replace a balanced diet, sunscreen, or medical care.
