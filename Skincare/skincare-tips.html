DIY skincare tip

Tomato & Honey Mask

Use this gentle rinse-off mask no more than 2-3 times a week. Do not scrub with the tomato; gentle application reduces friction and irritation.

  1. Patch test

    Test the mixture on a small area of the inner arm and wait 24 hours before using it on your face.

  2. Mix

    Combine 1 teaspoon fresh tomato pulp or juice with 1 teaspoon plain honey in a clean bowl.

  3. Apply gently

    Spread a thin layer over clean, dry skin. Avoid the eyes, lips, broken skin, and irritated areas.

  4. Rinse

    Leave on for 5 minutes the first time, up to 10 minutes if well tolerated. Rinse with cool water and moisturize.

Use with care

Tomato is acidic and can cause stinging, redness, dryness, or contact dermatitis. Stop immediately and rinse if it burns or itches. Avoid this mask if you have very sensitive skin, rosacea, eczema, a tomato allergy, or a bee-product allergy.

DIY tomato masks are not proven treatments for acne, dark spots, or other skin conditions. Use broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher every day, and do not combine this mask with exfoliating acids or retinoids that day.

Morning drink

Fennel, Cumin & Ajwain Water

A warm, aromatic seed infusion. Start with a few mornings per week; if it agrees with you, it can become part of your morning routine.

  1. Measure

    Use 1/2 teaspoon each fennel seeds (saunf), cumin seeds (jeera), and carom seeds (ajwain).

  2. Soak

    Add the seeds to 2 cups water, cover, and soak overnight in the refrigerator.

  3. Warm

    In the morning, simmer the seeds and water gently for 5 minutes. Do not reduce it heavily.

  4. Strain + sip

    Strain and drink 1 warm cup with or after breakfast. Refrigerate the remainder and use within 24 hours.

Keep expectations realistic

This infusion may feel soothing after meals, but it is not a proven detox, weight-loss method, or skincare treatment. Stop if it worsens heartburn, nausea, cramping, or diarrhea.

Check with a clinician before regular use if you are pregnant or breastfeeding, take prescription medicine, have a bleeding disorder, or are allergic to fennel, celery, carrot, or related plants.
