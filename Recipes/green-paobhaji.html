Mumbai classic, greener

Green Pav Bhaji

A vibrant mash of cauliflower, potato, peas, and bell pepper finished with spinach, coriander, mint, pav bhaji masala, and lemon.

Serves 4  |  Prep 20 min  |  Cook 30 min

Ingredients

For the green purée

  • 3 packed cups baby spinach
  • 1 packed cup coriander leaves and tender stems
  • 1/4 cup mint leaves
  • 1-2 green chilies
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice

For the bhaji

  • 2 medium potatoes, peeled and cubed
  • 2 cups cauliflower florets
  • 1 cup green peas
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil
  • 2 tbsp butter, divided
  • 1 medium onion, finely chopped
  • 1 green bell pepper, finely chopped
  • 1 tbsp ginger-garlic paste
  • 2 tbsp pav bhaji masala
  • 1/2 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp salt, or to taste
  • 1-1 1/2 cups hot water, as needed

To serve

  • 8 pav rolls
  • Butter for toasting
  • Chopped onion, coriander, and lemon wedges

Method

  1. Bring a pot of water to a boil. Add the spinach for 30 seconds, then immediately transfer it to ice water. Drain and gently squeeze out excess water.
  2. Blend the blanched spinach, coriander, mint, green chilies, lemon juice, and 2-3 tablespoons water into a smooth, bright green purée. Set aside.
  3. Place the potatoes, cauliflower, and peas in a pot with 1 cup water. Cover and cook for 12-15 minutes, until very tender. Mash roughly, keeping the cooking liquid.
  4. Heat the oil and 1 tablespoon butter in a wide pan. Cook the onion and bell pepper over medium heat for 6-8 minutes, until very soft. Add the ginger-garlic paste and cook for 1 minute.
  5. Add the pav bhaji masala and cumin with a splash of water. Stir for 30 seconds, then add the mashed vegetables and salt.
  6. Mash and stir over medium-low heat for 5-7 minutes, adding hot water gradually until the bhaji is thick, smooth, and spoonable.
  7. Lower the heat and stir in the green purée. Simmer gently for only 2-3 minutes. Turn off the heat and mix in the remaining 1 tablespoon butter. Taste and adjust salt and lemon.
  8. Split the pav without cutting all the way through. Toast cut-side down in butter until golden. Serve hot with bhaji, onion, coriander, and lemon wedges.

Keep it green

Blanching and cooling the spinach sets its bright color. Add the green purée only after the vegetables are fully cooked, then simmer briefly; prolonged cooking will make the bhaji olive-colored.

Store + reheat

Refrigerate bhaji in an airtight container for up to 3 days. Reheat gently with a splash of water and avoid boiling for long. Freeze for up to 1 month, though the green color may soften after reheating.
