Architecture Field Guide

Ancient Greece · 700-146 BCE

Classical Greek

Symmetry, mathematical proportion, pediments, and Doric, Ionic, or Corinthian columns.

Look at: The Parthenon, Athens.

Roman world · 509 BCE-476 CE

Roman

Concrete construction, arches, vaults, domes, aqueducts, and monumental civic interiors.

Look at: The Pantheon, Rome.

Europe · 10th-12th centuries

Romanesque

Heavy masonry, round arches, thick walls, small windows, towers, and rhythmic arcades.

Look at: Durham Cathedral, England.

Europe · 12th-16th centuries

Gothic

Pointed arches, ribbed vaults, flying buttresses, stained glass, and strong vertical emphasis.

Look at: Chartres Cathedral, France.

7th century-present

Islamic

Courtyards, domes, minarets, geometric ornament, calligraphy, muqarnas, and filtered light.

Look at: The Alhambra, Granada.

Europe · 15th-17th centuries

Renaissance

Classical orders revived through clear geometry, balanced facades, central plans, and domes.

Look at: Tempietto, Rome.

Europe and Americas · 17th-18th centuries

Baroque

Dramatic curves, theatrical space, layered ornament, grand axes, and controlled natural light.

Look at: San Carlo alle Quattro Fontane, Rome.

Japan · 16th-19th centuries

Traditional Japanese

Timber frames, modular tatami planning, deep eaves, sliding screens, gardens, and material restraint.

Look at: Katsura Imperial Villa, Kyoto.

International · 1920s-1970s

Modernism

Functional plans, minimal ornament, open interiors, glass walls, and expressed concrete or steel.

Look at: Villa Savoye, Poissy.

International · 1920s-1930s

Art Deco

Stepped silhouettes, streamlined geometry, rich materials, metallic details, and stylized ornament.

Look at: Chrysler Building, New York.

International · 1950s-1970s

Brutalism

Monumental massing, exposed concrete, repetitive modules, deep shadows, and visible structure.

Look at: Barbican Estate, London.

Global · 1980s-present

Sustainable Design

Passive climate response, low-carbon materials, adaptive reuse, efficient systems, and biodiversity.

Look at: Bullitt Center, Seattle.
